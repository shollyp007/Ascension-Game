Capstone Project · Design Document v0.1

Ascension

Climb the mountain above the village — on the word of God, or not at all.

A single-player, story-driven game in Unreal Engine 5. The player takes up one of three lives — a prophet, a high-school student, or a nurse — and climbs a mountain that rises over a village. Each stage of the climb is a trial set by the Village Master and worked against by tempting spirits. The trials are won not with weapons but with Scripture: the player who knows the word of God, and how to apply it, advances; the player who does not, falls. The tempters know the word too, and will quote it out of shape — as in Matthew 4.

Scope: an honest reckoning

As written, the full concept — three playable characters, a realistic village, an ascending series of levels, a dialogue system, spirit AI, a Scripture-knowledge mechanic, save and progression, and win/lose states with a prison — is two to three years of work for a team. Any one of those is a semester.

The capstone should be one vertical slice: a single path through the game, built and polished end to end, with the rest designed on paper and shown as concept.

Build for the capstone

  • One character — the high-school student (simplest to model, rig, and dress)
  • One environment — the village and the first stretch of mountain
  • One trial, fully playable — the trial of the flesh (seduction)
  • Character-select menu, a faith / resolve meter, pass & fail states
  • The prison scene and a working save file

Design on paper, show as concept

  • The money and the wealth / status trials
  • The prophet and the nurse
  • Levels beyond the first
  • The Village Master as a character
  • Multiplayer or player-vs-player of any kind

A capstone rewards one thing that works and is documented far above five things that are broken. The slice can be framed as “Act I” of a larger vision.

What the Unreal MCP actually gives you

The Unreal MCP servers let a language model drive the editor through Python and Remote Control. Be clear-eyed about the division of labour.

Good for

  • Greyboxing and layout — a village street grid, fences, props, in bulk
  • Populating the scene with NPCs and set dressing
  • Scaffolding Blueprints and UMG widgets, wiring simple logic
  • Repetitive edits — retag every spirit, batch-set materials
  • Procedural placement through editor Python

Will not do

  • Realism — that comes from Fab / Megascans assets, Nanite, Lumen, PCG foliage, and hours of lighting
  • Art direction and mood
  • Gameplay feel and balance

Workflow: buy a complete village kit → assemble and populate it with MCP → scaffold the gameplay Blueprints with MCP → art-direct the lighting and composition by hand.

Technical architecture

  • Engine: Unreal Engine 5.4+, built on the Third Person template.
  • Logic: Blueprints for nearly everything. Drop to C++ only when you hit a wall — a capstone timeline has no room for a hand-built C++ framework.

Core systems

  • Character selection — a menu level that writes a SelectedCharacter value into a GameInstance so it survives level loads.
  • Trial / encounter — a BP_Trial actor with a trigger volume. On overlap it freezes movement and starts a dialogue encounter.
  • Dialogue & choice UI — use a plugin (DlgSystem, Not-Yet-Dialogue, or Dialogue Tree). Do not hand-roll this.
  • Scripture knowledge check — the heart of the game (Section IV).
  • Resolution — a pass raises the faith stat and opens the path up; a fail teleports to the prison level and shows a game-over screen with the relevant verse and an invitation to try again.
  • Save — an Unreal SaveGame object holding character, current level, faith stat, and trials passed.
  • Spirits — no real combat AI. They are dialogue actors that patrol a spline or run a simple Behavior Tree; their attack is verbal — they approach and open a trial.

The core mechanic — Scripture encounters

The design anchor is the temptation of Christ (Matthew 4 / Luke 4). Satan quotes Scripture — “It is written” — and Jesus answers with better-fitted Scripture — “It is also written.” That is the combat system.

  1. The tempter makes a proposition and backs it with a real but misapplied verse.
  2. The player is given three or four responses. One is a correctly applied Scripture; the others are plausible worldly reasoning or misused verses.
  3. A right answer drops the tempter’s influence meter. A wrong answer drops the player’s resolve meter.
  4. Three to five exchanges per encounter. Resolve runs out → the player falls → prison. The tempter’s influence breaks → the player ascends.

Content pipeline

Build a Data Table where each row is one exchange: the tempter’s line, the misused reference, four options each flagged bIsCorrect, and an explanation shown afterward whatever the outcome — so the player learns, which is the point. The content is authored in a spreadsheet, kept separate from code, and can be handed to a pastor or advisor for review.

Get the Scripture content reviewed by someone qualified in your tradition. A game meant to teach the Bible cannot afford to misquote or misapply it — and an examiner may notice.

The three first-layer trials

Frame the trials as the triad of 1 John 2:16. This gives the level ladder a scriptural spine to cite in the written report.

Lust of the flesh

Seduction

Tempting spirits press the player toward fornication through flattery and proposition. Verbal and symbolic only — never depicted.

Proverbs 7 · 1 Cor 6:18 · 2 Tim 2:22

Lust of the eyes

Money

Gain by compromise — a bribe, a theft framed as need, a lie that pays. The offer looks like provision.

1 Tim 6:9–10 · Prov 30:8–9 · Matt 6:24

Pride of life

Wealth & status

Recognition, power, a name. “All this I will give you” — the summit offered without the climb.

Matt 4:8–10 · Jer 9:23–24 · James 4:6

Handling the seduction theme

  • Verbal and symbolic, never depicted. The temptation is the dialogue and the choice, not any imagery. The tempter flatters and propositions in text; the player answers.
  • On failure: fade to black, then the prison scene with a verse framed as instruction, not punishment.
  • No nudity, no sexual content, no suggestive animation. This holds a teen-appropriate rating, stays defensible in an academic setting, and makes a better teaching tool.

Milestone plan

Assuming roughly 13–14 weeks, solo. Cut ruthlessly if the schedule slips — the menu, one trial, pass / fail, and the write-up are the non-negotiable core.

Next steps & open questions

  • Choose and set up one Unreal MCP server.
  • Choose the dialogue plugin.
  • Buy or find the village environment kit; decide the art target — stylised realism or full realism (this affects everything downstream).
  • Write the full seduction-trial script — five exchanges — and get it reviewed.
  • Decide: does the faith meter carry between trials, or reset for each one?
  • Define the four deliverables — the playable slice, this design document, a demo video, the written report.
